01 / ABOUT

Following the questions.

My mission is to develop engineering platforms that can enable scientists to ask fundamental questions about the biological and physical world with greater precision.

Some facts about me: I grew up in Bareilly, northern India, attending a small neighborhood school without functioning science laboratories or a well-stocked library. I spent much of my childhood borrowing my mother's old fiction books, and at 17, bought my first laptop to explore machine learning and genetic engineering. At 18, I became the first person in my close family to leave the country to study in the United States, supported by scholarships and grants.

Research & projects

Developing methods for bacteriophage engineering

Why I chose this project

Bacteriophages are the most evolutionarily diverse species on the planet and provide an efficient chassis for testing molecular engineering strategies, helping me develop a modular understanding of protein and genome design.

Joseph Bondy-Denomy Lab @ UCSF | George Church Lab @ Harvard

Developing methods for mitochondrial engineering

Why I chose this project

The domestication of archaea in ancient bacteria allowed for the rise of multi-cellular species because of the development of an internal control node, today known as the mitochondria. Engineering it can unlock potential for longevity, neuroplasticity, and more.

Computational collaboration @ MIT/Harvard

Building microfluidic devices for vascular engineering

Why I chose this project

I wanted to apply the methods I learned in molecular cloning to tissue-engineering problems in healthcare and interface more with biophysics, through endothelial mechanobiology.

Christopher Chen Lab @ BU & Wyss Institute at Harvard
